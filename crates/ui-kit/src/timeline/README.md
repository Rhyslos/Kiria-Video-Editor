# timeline

The timeline widget (`../timeline.rs`) and its parts, one file each.

## What it does

The timeline widget every page shares: ruler with timecode, labels row, video area (V1 at the bottom) and audio area that scroll separately, a draggable divider, the playhead, the red edit needle (dragged by its handle), the scroll bar.

Moving around is the same over the ruler and both areas: Ctrl+wheel or a pinch zooms time at the pointer, Shift+wheel or a sideways wheel pans, the plain wheel scrolls the area under the pointer (and pans over the ruler and the scroll bar), a middle-button drag pans both ways; `ViewStep` is how a command zooms in or out. Zoomed all the way in a frame is 40 points wide and the ruler marks every frame.

Every track has its own height (`TrackHeights`, view state the page keeps): drag the edge of its name (the bottom edge of an audio track, the top edge of a video track), double-click it for the usual height, or Alt+wheel for every track of the area under the pointer.

Clips whose waveform or pictures are still being read say so (`Loading`: a slim bar with a percentage and the time left on the clip, the waveform filling in from the left, a short line by the track's name, "no waveform" when it failed). The top edge of the labels row is the cache bar (`CacheBar`): ready, being prepared and waiting stretches from ranges the page passes in, with a tooltip for the colours. Draws only what is visible and merges clips too thin to tell apart (`docs/research/timeline.md`); returns `TimelineEvent`s.

Tools: Select (click, drag to move, drag an edge to trim) Blade (click to cut), Ripple (trims move everything after), Roll (drag the cut between two touching clips), Slip (drag inside a clip to show another part of its file) and Slide (drag a clip between its neighbours); snapping to clip edges, the playhead, the needle and labels (Alt held while dragging skips it; an Alt+click moves the playhead instead).

Files dragged from a list (`DraggedMedia`) show where they'd land and drop as `DropMedia`; timelines dragged from the timeline tabs (`DraggedTimeline`) the same way, dropping as `DropTimeline`. An effect dragged from the media drawer (`DraggedEffect`) outlines the clip under the pointer and drops onto it as `DropEffect`.

What a clip plays (`ClipShows`) is drawn on it: a small stack of two frames in the top right corner of the part in view for a nested timeline (a double-click, or "Open its timeline" in its menu, sends `OpenNested`), light stripes for an adjustment layer. The right-click menu of the tracks also adds an adjustment layer on the video track under the pointer (`AddAdjustment`) and, with clips selected, nests them (`NestSelected`), and with two or more, lines them up by sound (`SyncBySound`).

Labels: click one to go to it, double-click to edit it (`EditLabel`). `highlights` marks stretches over every track (the silences Remove silence found); `spans` (`TimelineSpan`) marks coloured stretches faintly over every track, more strongly over the audio tracks each names, with a bar along the labels row whose tip says what it is (the clarity check's findings).

Video clips draw their pictures (`ClipPictures`) side by side, each the one nearest its place in the file, with the name on a dark pill. A clip whose speed changes over time hands in where in its file it is along it (`SourceMap`), which its pictures and waveform follow, and its speed (`SpeedCurve`), drawn as a thin line over its lower part. Audio clips draw their waveform (one bar per pixel column); audio track headers have the level button (a line across each clip at its volume: drag it for that clip's volume, double-click for 0 dB, `ClipVolume`; which tracks show it is view state, `level_lines`, and `level_being_set` tells other panels about a drag in progress), M (mute), S (solo) and a volume fader (double-click for 0 dB); waveforms grow and shrink with the clip's and the track's volume, live while the fader moves, and are dimmed on a muted track.

Any number of tracks of each kind: a clip dragged up or down lands on the track of its kind under the pointer (`Move` with a `track`; it is drawn there while dragged, and stays on its own track until the pointer is clearly past the row's edge), and a dragged file drops on the video track under the pointer. Dragged clearly past the last track of its kind (above the top video track, under the bottom audio track) a clip asks for a new track (`Move` with `new_track`): a lit strip named "New track" shows where, with the clip in it. When the tracks fill their area to its edge, so there is no room past the last, the band along that edge (`NEW_TRACK_EDGE`, 12 points) or past it asks, where the strip is drawn over the edge. A clip dragged to where another clip is in its way is drawn in the warning colour, with the "not allowed" pointer, before it is let go.

The video area is as tall as the divider says or, when that is more, as its tracks need, while the audio area keeps three tenths of the height (`fit_video`, `AUDIO_KEEPS`): an added track shows without dragging the divider and a removed one gives its room back; a track that was just added is scrolled into view.

The column of track names has a right-click menu (`AddTrack`, `RemoveTrack`, `HideTrack`, and Rename…), a video track's header has an eye that hides it (its clips are drawn dimmed), and a double-click on a name types a new one in place (`RenameTrack`).

`rough_cut` gives taller video rows with each clip's pictures on top and its sound below. `compact` draws every track as a strip `COMPACT_TRACK` (18) points tall with each clip's colour and name, the track's name, the eye and M / S (no pictures, waveforms, fades, level lines or fader), leaving the heights given to tracks as they are (resizing waits while it is on). `divider` off makes the video and audio tracks one list that scrolls together (video on top, audio under it, a hairline between them, nothing to drag); `split` and each area's scroll wait for it to come back.

A clip's `keyframes` (`KeyframeMark`s, one per frame with keyframes, saying which numbers are keyed there; the page fills them in for the selected clip) are small diamonds along its bottom edge, drawn whole inside the clip even on its first and last frame, with a tooltip naming the numbers: a click moves the playhead there (`Seek`), Shift-click or Ctrl-click chooses several, a drag that starts in the strip along the clip's bottom edge away from a mark draws a box that chooses the marks in it (Shift or Ctrl: added to those chosen; higher up the clip a drag moves the clip as before), `chosen_keyframes` says which are chosen (what the page's Copy copies) and `choose_keyframes` chooses some (the marks just pasted), a drag moves the chosen ones a frame at a time (snapping to the playhead and the clip's other marks unless snapping is off or Alt is held, never off the clip, shown refused where a number would get two keyframes in a frame; a hollow diamond stays where it was), and a right-click menu goes there, removes their keyframes or picks how they go on (`data.keyframe_eases`). Each is one `Keyframes` event (`MarksChange`); `keyframes_being_moved` tells the page about a drag in progress, so the viewer can show it.

`click_moves` (`ClickMoves`) says what a click or drag in the ruler moves: the playhead, or the edit needle when the page has one (it is then put down where the drag is let go); the playhead's head at the top of the ruler is a handle that drags the playhead either way (not with Shift, which begins a region), drawn larger with grip lines when a click moves the needle. Alt+click anywhere on the timeline (ruler, labels row, empty track space or a clip, any tool) sends a `Seek` to the start of the frame under the pointer whatever `click_moves` says, never selecting, cutting or trimming; it is a click as egui tells it (let go without dragging), so Alt+drag keeps its meaning on clips, edges and keyframe marks (no snapping), while Alt+drag in the ruler or on empty track space scrubs the playhead a frame at a time.

`toolbar` draws the tool row from `ToolbarClick::ALL` (each button knows its picture, its name and when it is lit, no tool while the lines are worked; the page gives each its tooltip); `folded_toolbar` offers the same buttons, Rough cut included, in one dropdown named after the tool in use (`folded_toolbar_text`, `Tool::name`) for a bar too narrow for the row; `lines_chip` is the lit "Lines on ×" pill a page puts after it while they are, a click (or the lines key, or Escape) leaving them.

Volume and pan lines (`lines`, `LinesShown`, set by the page): a track's line across its row and a clip's own inside it, drawn with their points when shown (faintly, without points, when they have some but are not shown); while shown the pointer over the audio tracks works them (click adds a point, drag moves it in whole frames between its neighbours, double-click or the right-click menu removes it, "Clear this line"), each change one `Line` event, `line_being_set` while dragging.

An audio track's role (`TimelineTrack::role`, an index into `TimelineData::roles`) is a mark in its colour by its name and is chosen in the names' right-click menu (`TrackRole`).

While a clip is dragged, what stays put is gathered once (`timeline/obstacles.rs`), so snapping and the "in the way" check cost a binary search per edge and per moving clip, not a pass over every clip; transitions and labels out of view are not drawn. `tests/timeline_cost.rs` measures a frame, and a frame of dragging every clip (`drag_everything_frame_cost`)

## How it is put together

`timeline.rs` holds what the page sees (`TimelineClip`, `TimelineTrack`, `TimelineData`, `TimelineEvent`, `TimelineView` and its view state), the `Layout` of the areas, the rows and scrolls, and the two entry points, each a short list of named steps:

- `show`: forget a gap that is gone, move the view (follow the playhead, commands, the wheel, the middle button), lay out, take the pointer (`input`, then `lines_input`), then draw: the areas (`draw_areas`: each track's edge, header and clips, a new track's strip), where something dragged in would land, the edges and divider, the ruler row, the page's spans, the region, the labels, the scroll bar, the playhead and needle.
- `input`: the ruler first, then the handles on it (playhead, needle, region; one that takes the pointer ends the frame's input), Alt+click, labels, ruler clicks, the divider, the scroll bar, the column of track names, and the clips of each area (`clip_input.rs`).

Small helpers stand for what was written out several times: `TimelineView::time_inside` (the time under a point, inside the timeline), `TimelineView::clip_rect` (where a clip is drawn in its row, at least a pixel wide), `TimelineTrack::clip_at` and `TimelineClip::covers` (the clip at a time) and `find_clip` (a clip by id).

The gestures in progress (a clip, marks, a box of marks, a level line, a line's point, the needle, the region, the scroll bar, a fader, a track's edge, a middle-button pan, a scrub) are still separate `Option` fields of `TimelineView`: only one is live at a time because of how the pointer is handled, not by their type.
