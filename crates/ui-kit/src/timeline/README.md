# timeline

Parts of the timeline widget (`../timeline.rs`) that stand on their own. The widget itself, its input and its drawing stay in `timeline.rs`.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `rows.rs` | `TrackHeights`: each track's height as a factor of the usual height of its kind (28 to 400 points), with what to save, a revision that counts changes, and `keep_only`, which puts the heights of removed tracks aside (they are not saved) and gives a track its height back when it returns, as it does when its removal is undone. `Rows`: the rows of one area for tracks of mixed heights (video rows stack upward, audio rows downward), which row a position is in, the nearest row to a position past the first or last (where a dragged clip or a dropped file lands), and how far the area can scroll |
| `fades.rs` | `ClipSound`: a clip's volume and fades, for drawing. The waveform is drawn as loud as the clip and its track are set (following the track's fader while it is dragged, never taller than the clip), and each fade as a line from a bottom corner to the top on the same quarter sine the mixer uses, with the faded part above it darkened |
| `level.rs` | The level line: with an audio track's level button on, a line across each of its clips at the clip's volume. Pure functions place it (`db_at`, `height_of`: 0 dB in the middle, straight decibels up to +12 dB at the top, and below the middle a curve with the same slope there that puts −6 dB a quarter of the way from the middle to the bottom, −12 dB at about 40 % of it, −24 dB at about 60 % and Off, −60 dB, at the bottom), `settled_db` (tenths of a decibel, 0 dB within half a decibel), `words` ("−3.0 dB", "Off"), `grabs` (4 points either side). `LevelDrag` follows the pointer by how far it moved, a fifth as far with Alt; the line and, while dragging, a tag with the value are drawn here; the timeline sends `ClipVolume` when it is let go or double-clicked (0 dB) |
| `track_views.rs` | Two ways of looking at the tracks the Display menu switches: `COMPACT_TRACK` (every track's height with compact tracks on) and `clip_inset`, and `OneList`, the sums for video and audio tracks as one list with the divider off: how far it scrolls, how much of the window the video rows take, and the scroll each area's rows are drawn with so both kinds lie end to end |
| `headers.rs` | The column of track names: its right-click menu (add a video or an audio track; rename, hide or remove the track under the pointer, show or hide an audio track's level lines, with the reason when it can't be removed), the eye in a video track's header that hides the track, where a header's small buttons go (shorter and centred in a compact header), and the field a name is typed in after a double-click (Enter or a click elsewhere gives the name, Escape leaves it) |
| `loading.rs` | `Loading`: background reading a clip or a track still waits for (sound or pictures; waiting, being read with the share done and the time left, or failed), what it says in a clip of any width and by a track's name, and how it is drawn (in a short clip, as on compact tracks, the bar is a thin line along its bottom edge) |
| `keyframes.rs` | Keyframe marks: `KeyframeMark` (a frame with keyframes: its start, the numbers keyed there for the tooltip and for telling whether two marks clash, the ease they share), small diamonds (`keyframe::paint_mark`) along the bottom edge of a clip, moved in from its edges so one on the first or last frame is drawn whole, left out of a clip too thin to hold them; `hit` finds the one under the pointer (the nearest of two close ones). `Chosen` (Shift-click adds and takes out), `MarkDrag` and the pure parts of a drag: `dragged_by` (snap within reach, whole frames, never off the clip, all chosen marks together), `frames_of` (a clip's first and last frame) and `clashes` (a number would get two keyframes in a frame). The timeline turns these into `Seek` and `Keyframes` events and the marks' right-click menu |
| `cache_bar.rs` | The cache bar under the ruler: `CacheState` (ready, preparing, waiting), `CacheRange`, `flatten` (one tidy list out of overlapping ranges; the least ready state wins), and drawing that skips what is out of view and gives ranges too thin to tell apart one shared span |
