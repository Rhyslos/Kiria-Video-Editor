# engine

Ties the parts together: what is on the timeline at a moment, and the workers that turn it into pictures, sound and exported files. The interface only sends requests and reads results back, so slow media can never freeze a window.

## Status

The picture at the playhead (every visible layer, put together on the graphics card), the timeline's sound (since 8.1 mixed through `crates/audio`: every track's effects, volume, pan, mute and solo, and the master, in the sound card's callback, with edits heard within a block while playing), playback with the sound as the clock, and export to every container and codec of version 1 (6.1). Export also scales to another size and frame rate, writes the sound beside as WAV, pauses between frames and measures a mix's loudness for the Export page (6.2). The clarity check (8.2) finds, on a worker, where another track covers the dialogue and where several pile up between 200 and 500 Hz, from each track's sound after its effects, in plain words with one-click fixes; the Audio page's middle band draws it.

The audio thread opens the default sound card (cpal, with its `realtime` feature: the callback runs at the priority the system gives sound; a refusal is logged) with a stereo stream when the card mixes more channels and takes one (`stream_channels`: handed as the card's own seven or eight channels, a virtual surround placed the mix as two speakers of many, quieter; see `docs/research/sound-quality.md`), else its own number; the log names the device, rate, channels and sample format; makes the real-time mixer for the timeline at the card's rate, fills a tenth of a second of every track and starts the stream; then it is the feeder, keeping every track's ring about half a second ahead. The position is the timeline sample the card is handed less the mixer's fixed delay (23 ms at 48 kHz), so the picture stays in step; it stands at the start (or the place jumped to) until that sound is ready. Without a sound card (none, or it won't open or start: the mix is handed back from the stream's slot) the same mixer runs unheard on a thread of its own at real-time pace (`output::Paced`, 48 kHz stereo), so the clock, the meters, the loudness count and ducking work as with sound; `unheard` says so (`start_unheard` never opens the card, for tests); when the card goes away while playing (an error that ends its stream, or its callback no longer called for a second) the stream is let go on a thread of its own, the mix comes back (`Held` puts it back in the slot when the callback is let go) and plays on unheard from where it was, at the card's rate, and a jump made meanwhile is made again then instead of waiting for the card; only when even the mix can't be made (or doesn't come back) does a wall clock run. `follow` (every frame of the interface while playing) sends the project when a track's mute, solo, volume or sound, the master's, or the tracks themselves changed (a comparison, nothing built otherwise), `update` sends it anyway: the feeder sends only what changed, heard within a block (undo too); tracks added while playing come in in step, to the sample. `jump` goes on from another place without opening the card again (the callback gives silence while the mixer takes `locate` and the feeder fills from there): dragging the playhead, looping, and a clip's sound changed. Stopping never waits for the audio thread: the card goes quiet at once and the thread ends by itself; closing the app does wait, briefly (`wait_until_quiet`), also for the unheard mix's `paced-sound` thread and a sound card being let go (each counted as an audio thread, `Running`). `levels` and `readout` read the master's meter; `track_meters` each track's (`audio::TrackReadout`, the list swapped by the audio thread when tracks come and go), `analyse` chooses what the analyser listens to (`Listen`: a track for the EQ, or the mix for the Audio page's band; stepped on the audio thread) and `spectrum` is what it shows (levels by band and the stereo picture) |

## Numbers

Playing 32 tracks with every effect on and the master's compressor and limiter, 32 files decoded by the feeder, a device thread asking for 256 frames every 5.3 ms (test build, two-core cloud machine; `thirty_two_tracks_play_through_the_real_time_path`, by hand): mean 0.86 to 1.04 ms a block in the callback, median about 0.9 ms, no frame late. The mix comes out 1111 frames (23.1 ms at 48 kHz) after it is fed, whatever is on; the clock takes that off.

A nested timeline costs the feeder what exporting it costs: for the three-track test recording with every effect on its tracks and master, about 1 to 1.5 ms of the feeder's thread per piece of 1 024 frames (21 ms of sound) while it sounds, 5 to 7 % of one core (test build, the same busy machine; `cost_of_a_nested_track`, run by hand with `--ignored --nocapture`); making its mixer (opening its files and mixing the 23 ms delay ahead) takes 6 to 40 ms once, when the clip is first reached and after each jump. Nothing of it runs in the callback. Mixed offline, very small numbers count as zero, as in the callback: without that, effects dying away in silence made an export about twenty times slower there (11 ms a piece against 0.6 ms).
