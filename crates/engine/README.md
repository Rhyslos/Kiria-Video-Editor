# engine

Ties the parts together: what is on the timeline at a moment, and the workers that turn it into pictures (and later sound and exported files). The interface only sends requests and reads results back, so slow media can never freeze a window.

## Status

The picture at the playhead (every visible layer, put together on the graphics card), the timeline's sound, playback with the sound as the clock, and export to every container and codec of version 1 (6.1). The rest of export (loudness, the full page, the queue) is Phase 6.

## Files

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `src/silence.rs` | `find`: the quiet stretches of a sound track from its waveform peaks (gaps count as silent), with a threshold, a minimum length and a little kept at each edge; a ramped clip's place in its file follows its ramp |
| `src/frames.rs` | `FrameCache`: frames kept after they were decoded, found by file and moment (a frame is on screen from its own time until the next one's), within a budget in bytes; room is made by other files' frames first, then by the frames furthest from the playhead (`look_at`). `stretch_start` says where the unbroken kept stretch around a moment begins and `would_keep` whether a frame would stay, for the worker's fill behind the playhead. `budget_on_card`: how much to keep on a graphics card of a given size. The preview worker keeps the frames decoded on the window's device here and looks here before decoding |
| `src/thumbnail.rs` | The project's picture for the launcher: one frame of the timeline (at the playhead, or the first clip's), fitted into 480×270 on black, written as `<file>.thumbnail.png`; `save_frame` writes the frame at a time at its file's full size |
| `src/source.rs` | What the Source half of a split viewer shows: `source` picks the selected clip with a picture (a selected sound clip stands for the picture linked to it) and the moment in its file at the playhead (`within`: its first or last frame when the playhead is outside it), following its speed; `file_alone` makes a project of that file alone, whole, at its own size and rate and looking as it is, so an ordinary preview worker asked for it shows the file raw, with its frame cache and everything else |
| `src/timeline.rs` | `video_at`: the top visible video clip at a time and where in its file that is; a nested timeline stands for the top clip it shows then, an adjustment layer for nothing. `DEEPEST`: how many timelines deep nesting is followed (16) |
| `src/frame.rs` | `describe`: everything that is seen at a time, as plain numbers: every video track that isn't hidden contributes its clip, bottom first, each fitted into the timeline's frame (bars, never a stretched or cut-off picture) and then placed, sized, turned, cut and coloured as its clip's look at that moment says (`Clip::look_at`: numbers with keyframes take their values there, so playback, the viewer and export all animate alike) (`placement`, `shown_part`, `colour`; `shown_area` is where what is seen of a picture lands, which the handles on the picture in the viewer outline). Layers that a frame-filling one above them hides are left out, so they are never decoded. A description is the pictures of files to decode (`layers`, in drawing order, wherever they are nested) and how they are put together (`steps`, bottom first): `Step::Picture`, `Step::Timeline` (a nested timeline: its own steps at its own size, then placed, cut, turned, coloured and faded by its clip's look as one picture; it hides what is below only when it fills the frame and nothing in it is see-through) and `Step::Adjust` (an adjustment layer: its colour and opacity over everything below it; left out when it changes nothing or has nothing below it). `is_flat` says there are only pictures. `at_size` gives the same picture at a preview's size. A clip with frame blending on gives its layer a `Blend` when the moment falls between two recorded frames (`blend_at`, worked out exactly from the file's frame rate): the next frame's time and its share of the mix; exactly on a recorded frame there is none |
| `src/export.rs` | `Export`: the timeline, or a stretch of it, written as one file on a worker. `ExportSettings` names the file, the stretch, the `Container` (MP4, MKV; WAV, MP3, OGG for sound alone), the `VideoCodec` (none for sound alone), the `AudioCodec`, the `Quality` and whether to keep to the processor; `check` says why a combination can't be (the lists, and which go together, are `media::encode`'s, re-exported here), `estimated_bytes` how large it comes to. `Quality` sets the bit rate from the size and frame rate (H.265 and AV1 take less than H.264 for the same picture; `video_bit_rate`) and the sound's per codec (`audio_bit_rate`; none for FLAC and WAV). `Encoders::detect` (or `detect_in_background`) says what this machine can encode: the graphics card's encoder that opened and the processor's for each picture codec, the encoder for each sound codec; `can` and `combinations` answer for a page. Each frame's picture comes from a preview worker of its own at the timeline's full size (decoded and put together exactly as in the viewer, from the recordings: never handed the proxies, and waiting for each exact frame, never one shown first), is read back as the planes an encoder takes (`render::Reader`), and goes to `media::encode::Writer` with the sound of the same stretch from the playback mixer, to the exact sample (at 48 kHz for Opus); the sound is measured as it is written (`Exported::loudness`, integrated, and `true_peak`). With `normalise` (a loudness to aim for) the whole stretch is mixed and measured first, without writing anything (`Progress::measuring`), then exported with the gain that brings it there (`Normalised`: what it measured and the gain; never more than 20 dB up, none for silence); with `limit` the master goes through `limiter::Limiter` at -1 dBTP (mixed ahead by its delay, so the sound still lines up to the sample; `Exported::limited_db`). Without either, the mix is written sample for sample as it plays. A sound-only file needs no graphics device. The file is written as `<name>.exporting.<ending>` and renamed when complete; cancelled or failed, nothing is left. `progress`, `cancel`, `finished`. Its tests check that a nested timeline and an adjustment layer are in the exported picture (and the nested timeline's sound in its sound), that a speed ramp exports exactly the recorded frames it reaches and that blended frames are mixed, on a recording whose frames show their own numbers (`media`'s `counted-120.mkv`); that a stretch starting between key frames starts on its exact frame; that the picture is the recording's at full size; that every combination this build encodes exports a recording's flash and beep together, at the right moment, with every frame; that normalising lands on the loudness aimed for with the true peak under -1 dBTP (as written and as read back), and that without it the file is the mix sample for sample, and the recording's sound streams each read straight through and added up |
| `src/compose.rs` | `Composer`: puts one moment's decoded pictures together into the timeline's picture, following the description's steps (`Drawing`): a nested timeline is put together first into a picture of its own, see-through where nothing is, and drawn as one layer; at an adjustment layer what is below it is put together into a picture of its own, which is drawn as it is and again, coloured, at the adjustment's opacity (why: its colour must apply to the picture below as a whole, in linear light, as a clip's own colour does; colouring each layer below would be wrong where they overlap or are see-through, and could not reach into a nested timeline). These pictures are kept and drawn into again for the next frame on the device the window is drawn with (`render::Compositor`, in linear light). A blended layer brings the next recorded frame too, drawn from a second set of textures and mixed in the compositor's shader (left out when it is stored differently). Planes and RGBA in memory are written into textures kept per layer; pictures decoded on the window's device are drawn from where they are (`Composing::wrap`). Finished frames (`Composed`) are drawn into again once nobody shows them. Why the worker does this and not the interface: `docs/research/rendering.md` |
| `src/preview.rs` | `Preview`: a worker thread that decodes the picture at the playhead. Only the newest request counts (scrubbing never queues), also while a picture is being decoded: a decode the newer request can't use is given up between frames, one that is on the way to it carries on, and the decode after one that was given up is always finished, so dragging still gets pictures; when nothing is asked for 80 ms it fills in the stretch behind the kept frames on a second decoder (`fill`), stopping the moment a request arrives; a request whose picture can't be had at once (not kept, not a few frames ahead of its decoder) first gets a picture that can (`stand_in`, `Picture::stand_in`): each such layer from its file's proxy (`set_proxies` hands the worker the `cache::proxy::Shelf`; the viewer's workers get it, an export's never) or else the key frame before the moment from a decoder of its own (`glance`; not when `set_key_frame_first(false)`, Settings' "Wait for the exact frame": then a file without a proxy shows nothing first), then the exact picture, whose decode a newer request may give up when a proxy answered (it gets a proxy frame at once too); nothing is shown first while playing (`set_playing`) or for a short step back without a proxy; with proxies On a file with a proxy is shown from it alone and its original never opened; the corner says "proxy frame" or "key frame before it"; decoders stay open for the four files used last; each file is decoded the fastest way that works for it and steps down when a way fails, at opening or at any picture (`ladder`: on the window's graphics device when the editor hands one over as `OnDevice`, then the GPU's decoder with a copy back, then the CPU), with the reason in the log and no error on screen unless the last way fails too; a file the window's device failed for is not tried there again in the session; `DeviceWatch` is told before the device is asked for anything and how it came back (answered, or an error), for the crash and freeze guard in `graphics`; errors come back with the picture. Started with the window's device (`start_composing`), the worker decodes every layer that is seen (`frame::describe`) and hands back the timeline's whole picture as one finished frame (`Picture::composed`); one layer behaves exactly as a picture alone does, several are each decoded to the end so they show the same moment. A layer with a `Blend` also gets the recorded frame after its own (`next_frame`: kept, or one more frame for its decoder), mixed in when composed; without the window's device there is no blending. Without the device the top clip's picture comes back alone (`Picture::frame`). `Convert` says where colour is converted: pictures come as YUV planes for the viewer's shader when it can take them (in memory, or as images on the graphics card when decoded on the window's device), else as RGBA. `close` stops the worker and waits, briefly, until it has ended: for closing the app, so nothing is still decoding when the window's graphics go away |
| `tests/jumps.rs` | A measurement run by hand (`JUMP_CLIP=clip.mp4 cargo test -p engine --test jumps -- --ignored --nocapture`): the time from a jump to its first picture and to its exact one, without a proxy, with one on Auto and with proxies On, and how fast the proxy builds; the numbers are in `docs/research/caching.md` |
| `src/loudness.rs` | `Loudness`: loudness and true peak measured as the international broadcast loudness recommendation and the loudness rule built on it describe: K-weighting (a high shelf and a high pass per channel, worked out for any rate), 100 ms blocks of mean square summed over the channels, momentary (0.4 s) and short-term (3 s) loudness, integrated loudness gated twice (blocks under -70 LUFS, then those 10 LU under the rest) from a histogram of 0.01 LU steps, so any length takes the same memory and measuring never allocates, and the true peak of each channel from four-times oversampling (a Kaiser-windowed sinc in four phases of 16 taps; the first phase is the samples themselves). `reset` starts the integrated loudness and the highest true peak again. Tested against the standard's reference tones (1 kHz at -23 dBFS in both channels reads -23 LUFS; at 0 dBFS in one channel -3.01; -36/-23/-36 dBFS for 10/60/10 s integrates to -23) and its true-peak tones (a quarter, sixth and eighth of the rate at phases where no sample is the peak read within +0.2/-0.4 dB) |
| `src/limiter.rs` | `Limiter`: a true-peak limiter for the master (stereo, linked): the true peak of each frame found as `loudness` finds it (four times oversampled), the gain it needs let go over 80 ms, the lowest over a short look-ahead held and averaged so the gain glides down in time for each peak and stays down across it, the sound delayed by `latency`. Aims 0.1 dB under its ceiling; allocates nothing after `new`; any block length gives the same sound. Tested: quiet sound passes untouched, a tone twice full scale, a tone whose samples miss its peaks, a click and loud noise all come out under -1 dBTP, a steady tone settles just under it, the gain comes back after a peak |
| `src/meter.rs` | `Meter`: what `Loudness` measures of the master output, fed by the audio thread and read by the interface without locks (`Reading`: sample and true peaks per channel, the highest true peak, momentary, short-term and integrated loudness); `Reading::reset` asks for the integrated loudness and highest true peak to start again (`Playback::reset_loudness`) |
| `src/mix.rs` | `Mixer`: the timeline's sound at any moment: each clip's own sound first (`volume` from thousandths of a decibel, the bottom of the range silent; `pan_gains`, equal power with the centre leaving the sound as it is; fades on a quarter sine, `fade`, sample-exact from the clip's start and end on the timeline whatever its speed; a clip left as recorded costs nothing), then audible tracks (mute, solo, volume) summed; a clip playing a nested timeline mixes that timeline with a mixer of its own (`nested_sound`, kept per timeline and rate), at the rate that makes it play at the clip's speed (tape-style; a ramp at its speed over each stretch mixed), decoders kept open per file, stream and reading rate (a clip at another speed reads its file faster or slower, tape-style), seeking only on a real jump: a stretch that is the same clip, unchanged, carrying on from the timeline sample where the last stopped, or a clip starting at the decoder's exact sample (a clip cut in two), is read on; rounding never makes it seek, so a clip mixed a block at a time is its file read straight, to the sample (tested on a recording with rounded Opus timestamps and on files whose sound starts before and after the picture, and at a speed whose reading rate is rounded). A clip with a speed ramp (`ramped`) reads its file at the mixer's own rate and takes each sample from where the ramp says the clip is then, between two read samples by their distance: tape-style too, the pitch following the speed as it changes; reading carries on from call to call (the last sample is kept for the step between them), so it only seeks when the clip doesn't continue |
| `src/playback.rs` | `Playback`: an audio thread owns the sound card stream and the mixer, keeping a quarter-second ring buffer full; the card's callback never waits or allocates. The position is the samples actually played (the master clock); without a sound card a wall clock runs and playback is silent. The clock stands still until the first sound is ready, so the picture never runs ahead of it, and stopping never waits for the audio thread: the card goes quiet at once and the thread ends by itself. Closing the app does wait, briefly, for those threads to end (`wait_until_quiet`) |
| `build.rs` | Linux debug builds: the tests find the fetched FFmpeg libraries |
