# engine

Ties the parts together: what is on the timeline at a moment, and the workers that turn it into pictures (and later sound and exported files). The interface only sends requests and reads results back, so slow media can never freeze a window.

## Status

The picture at the playhead (every visible layer, put together on the graphics card), the timeline's sound (since 8.1 mixed through `crates/audio`: every track's effects, volume, pan, mute and solo, and the master, in the sound card's callback, with edits heard within a block while playing), playback with the sound as the clock, and export to every container and codec of version 1 (6.1). Export also scales to another size and frame rate, writes the sound beside as WAV, pauses between frames and measures a mix's loudness for the Export page (6.2). The clarity check (8.2) finds, on a worker, where another track covers the dialogue and where several pile up between 200 and 500 Hz, from each track's sound after its effects, in plain words with one-click fixes; the Audio page's middle band draws it.

## Numbers

Playing 32 tracks with every effect on and the master's compressor and limiter, 32 files decoded by the feeder, a device thread asking for 256 frames every 5.3 ms (test build, two-core cloud machine): mean 0.86 to 1.04 ms a block in the callback, median about 0.9 ms, no frame late. The mix comes out 1111 frames (23.1 ms at 48 kHz) after it is fed, whatever is on; the clock takes that off.

A nested timeline costs the feeder what exporting it costs: for the three-track test recording with every effect on its tracks and master, about 1 to 1.5 ms of the feeder's thread per piece of 1 024 frames (21 ms of sound) while it sounds, 5 to 7 % of one core (test build, the same busy machine; `cost_of_a_nested_track`, run by hand with `--ignored --nocapture`); making its mixer (opening its files and mixing the 23 ms delay ahead) takes 6 to 40 ms once, when the clip is first reached and after each jump. Nothing of it runs in the callback. Mixed offline, very small numbers count as zero, as in the callback: without that, effects dying away in silence made an export about twenty times slower there (11 ms a piece against 0.6 ms).
